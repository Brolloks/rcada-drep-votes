## RCADA Rationale

RCADA votes **YES** on **Update Constitutional Committee 2026**.

RCADA supports this action because it implements the audited outcome of the 2026 Constitutional Committee election and helps maintain a functioning Constitutional Committee as existing seats reach the end of their terms.

The Constitutional Committee is a core part of Cardano’s governance system. Its role is important for constitutional review, governance continuity, and the proper functioning of on-chain decision-making. As committee seats expire, they must be renewed through a legitimate process so that the committee remains operational and able to perform its duties.

RCADA recognises that this action does not change the committee quorum, does not alter the remaining seats, and does not modify the Constitutional Committee structure. It updates the four seats expiring at Epoch 653 by appointing the candidates elected through the 2026 Constitutional Committee election for terms ending at Epoch 799.

RCADA participated in the Ekklesia voting process. Not all candidates supported by RCADA made the final cut, but RCADA respects the audited election outcome. A governance system must be able to move from election results to implementation, even where individual voters or DReps would have preferred a different final composition.

RCADA also notes the strong turnout among DReps who participated in the Ekklesia Constitutional Committee election process. The published election interface shows 37 of 37 participating DRep voters took part, with almost all expressed votes cast rather than abstained. This supports respecting the audited election result, while not removing broader concerns about overall DRep participation, voting-power concentration, and the need to keep improving future election processes.

RCADA therefore supports this action on the basis of process legitimacy, continuity, and respect for the completed election. The proposal describes a process involving candidate registration, a public DRep voting period, a voting-power snapshot, independent audit, and publication of the audited result before submission of the on-chain action.

RCADA also recognises the timing complexity described in the proposal. Because the new members’ expiration epoch is fixed at Epoch 799 and the maximum term length is 146 epochs, the action is structured to enact at Epoch 653. While this is counterintuitive, RCADA accepts that this follows from the current ledger rules and does not change the substance of the vote.

RCADA’s YES vote should not be read as a personal endorsement of every individual committee member or as a statement that the election process cannot be improved. Future Constitutional Committee elections should continue to improve candidate visibility, community participation, decentralisation, accessibility, transparency, and resilience against voting-power concentration.

On balance, RCADA supports this action because it respects the audited election result, renews expiring Constitutional Committee seats, and helps preserve continuity in Cardano governance.
