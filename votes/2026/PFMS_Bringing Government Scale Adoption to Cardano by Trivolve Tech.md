## Summary

The **Police Force Management System (PFMS)** proposal by Trivolve Tech requests **₳9,600,000** from the Cardano Treasury to develop and deploy an open-source, blockchain-supported evidence-management infrastructure over approximately **15 months**.

The proposed system would establish verifiable records of evidence creation, handling, storage, transfer and presentation throughout the criminal justice process, using Cardano mainnet to anchor cryptographic evidence fingerprints.

The initiative proposes an initial government-scale deployment in **Telangana, India**, building on Trivolve Tech's existing Forensic Management System (FMS) experience.

Potential ecosystem benefits include:

- Government-scale adoption of Cardano infrastructure.
- Approximately **1.5 million projected annual mainnet transactions** from the initial deployment.
- Reusable open-source evidence-verification software.
- Increased use of decentralised identity and cryptographic verification.
- A proposed allocation of **70% of optional paid-verification revenue to the Cardano Treasury**, with 20% allocated to core Cardano library maintenance and 10% to Trivolve Tech.
- Government-funded ongoing transaction fees, maintenance and operational infrastructure, according to the proposal.

RCADA recognises the project's potential contribution to practical blockchain adoption, institutional accountability and long-term ecosystem development.

However, several material questions remain insufficiently resolved for RCADA to provide an affirmative vote, particularly concerning **government contractual commitments, integration with existing criminal justice infrastructure and protection of the substantial initial advance payment**.

**RCADA therefore ABSTAINS, recognising the initiative's potential while providing constructive feedback on the additional assurances needed to support a Treasury withdrawal of this magnitude.**

## Key Considerations

- **Existing technical foundation:** Trivolve Tech reports an operational FMS implementation on Cardano with substantial recorded mainnet activity. This provides a more developed starting point than an entirely conceptual proposal.

- **Government-scale adoption opportunity:** The proposed Telangana deployment could provide a substantial real-world use case for Cardano, subject to verification of the government's commitments and implementation readiness.

- **Potential public benefit:** Stronger evidence integrity and custody records could contribute to more accountable criminal justice procedures.

- **Reusable infrastructure:** The proposal describes an open-source evidence layer intended to support future deployments beyond the initial government implementation.

- **Treasury revenue participation:** The proposed 70% share of optional paid-verification revenue is a constructive attempt to provide an ongoing financial benefit to the ecosystem. However, future revenue is not guaranteed.

- **Contractual transparency:** The proposer states that government agreements exist and have been reviewed by the Cardano Foundation, but that they cannot be publicly disclosed because of confidentiality requirements. Their detailed obligations have not been independently verified in this assessment.

- **Existing-system integration:** Further clarity is needed regarding the relationship between PFMS and established Indian evidence-management systems, including e-Sakshya, and applicable electronic-evidence requirements.

- **Advance-payment exposure:** The initial **₳1,800,000 kickoff payment represents 18.75% of the total requested withdrawal**, before acceptance of the first substantive development milestone.

- **Independent assurance:** The independent assurer is expected to be procured after initial funding. The timing and enforceability of associated protections require clarification.

- **Impact verification:** Projected transactions, paid-verification revenues and improvements in case-resolution efficiency require transparent assumptions and independent measurement.

## What this action does

The proposal requests a **₳9.6 million Treasury withdrawal** to fund the design, development, integration, testing and deployment of PFMS across five stages of the criminal justice evidence lifecycle.

### Proposed Evidence Lifecycle

1. **Pre-Crime Evidence:** Recording and preserving relevant evidence from operational information sources.

2. **Crime Investigation:** Capturing and verifying evidence created during investigations.

3. **Evidence Storage:** Maintaining records of physical and digital evidence custody, including storage and transfer events.

4. **Prosecution:** Supporting verifiable evidence records and documentation used in criminal proceedings.

5. **Courts:** Enabling evidence-integrity verification when records are presented or challenged in court.

### Core Technical Architecture

The proposal describes six shared technical engines:

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Source Engine | Identifies and standardises records from different evidence sources |
| Capture Engine | Creates cryptographic fingerprints and supports offline capture and later submission |
| Identity Engine | Associates devices and authorised personnel with verifiable identities and credentials |
| Stamping Engine | Signs and groups evidence fingerprints for integrity verification |
| Commit Engine | Anchors cryptographic evidence commitments on Cardano mainnet |
| Verification Engine | Checks evidence against recorded cryptographic commitments |

The intended architecture keeps sensitive evidence content within authorised government systems while anchoring cryptographic fingerprints on Cardano.

This may help demonstrate whether evidence has changed after its recorded capture. It does not independently establish that the original evidence was genuine, lawfully obtained or legally admissible.

### Process & Governance Quality

#### Positive Features

The proposal contains several governance arrangements that RCADA welcomes:

- A named Treasury administrator.
- A multisignature custody structure for administrator-held Treasury funds.
- Milestone-based payments and independently reviewed deliverables.
- Published work packages and implementation milestones.
- An allocation for security testing, audit and administration.
- Commitments to return unspent or undisbursed funding under the specified conditions.
- A proposed public reporting dashboard for milestone progress and disbursements.

These measures establish an accountability framework appropriate to the proposed scale of development.

#### Government Contractual Commitments

The proposal states that Telangana government agreements exist and have been reviewed by the Cardano Foundation but cannot be disclosed publicly because of confidentiality obligations.

RCADA recognises that public-sector technology agreements may legitimately contain sensitive information.

Nevertheless, the commitments are central to the project's value proposition.

We would welcome a non-confidential, independent assurance confirming:

- The existence and enforceability of the relevant agreements.
- The authorised scope of the Telangana deployment.
- The government's responsibilities regarding transaction fees and infrastructure.
- Maintenance responsibilities, contractual duration and continuation arrangements.
- Any conditions that could prevent or materially delay full deployment.

Such confirmation would provide greater confidence without requiring disclosure of sensitive government information.

#### Existing Government Systems and Legal Integration

PFMS is proposed for use within an established criminal justice environment.

RCADA would welcome further details regarding integration with India's national and state evidence-management systems, including e-Sakshya and related police information systems.

Key considerations include:

- Technical interoperability with existing evidence capture and case-management processes.
- Avoidance of unnecessary duplication.
- Legal recognition and admissibility of electronically verified evidence.
- Privacy, access controls and protection of sensitive information.
- The rights of defendants and other affected parties to independently challenge or verify evidence.
- Responsibilities for resolving inconsistencies between PFMS and existing government records.

A cryptographic record can improve tamper detection, but it cannot independently resolve all legal and procedural questions surrounding the original collection and handling of evidence.

#### Initial Treasury Advance

The initial kickoff payment of **₳1.8 million** is particularly significant.

It represents **18.75% of the total request** and is scheduled for release upon administrator contract signing, before the first substantive milestone has been accepted.

The independent assurer is also expected to be procured after the initial tranche is released.

RCADA would welcome clarification of:

- Contractual protections attached to the advance.
- Independent verification required before the first payment.
- Remedies for failure to deliver or abandonment of scope.
- Recovery mechanisms for improperly used or unearned funds.
- The treatment of implementation costs if government deployment is delayed or cannot proceed.
- Whether independent assurance could be established before material implementation payments are made.

**Governance assessment: Constructive framework with material outstanding questions concerning contractual assurance and advance-payment protection.**

### Impact & Risk Analysis

#### 1. Cardano Adoption and Technical Capability

The existing FMS implementation provides a technical foundation for the proposed PFMS expansion.

The project could contribute to Cardano adoption through:

- Institutional use of blockchain verification.
- Increased transaction activity.
- Reusable evidence-management components.
- Decentralised identity infrastructure.
- Integration with public-sector information systems.
- Wider recognition of Cardano as an institutional trust layer.

However, the proposed expansion from an existing application to a comprehensive state-scale justice infrastructure is a substantial undertaking.

The success of the earlier system is relevant evidence of capability, but it does not remove the need to verify the new deployment's readiness, contracts and implementation requirements.

#### 2. Transaction Volume

The proposal projects approximately **1.53 million annual Cardano transactions** from the initial Telangana deployment.

This is equivalent to approximately **127,500 transactions per month**.

The proposal describes these figures as estimates based on expected operational records and batching requirements.

RCADA would welcome transparent supporting assumptions and a methodology for measuring actual usage once deployed.

The proposed percentage increase in Cardano network activity should also be reassessed against current network transaction levels rather than relying on a historical baseline.

#### 3. Treasury Revenue Model

The proposed optional paid-verification service allocates revenue as follows:

| Beneficiary | Proposed Share |
|---|---:|
| Cardano Treasury | **70%** |
| Core Cardano library maintenance | **20%** |
| Trivolve Tech | **10%** |
| **Total** | **100%** |

The proposal describes a starting paid-verification charge of **US$1.89**, with higher charges for more complex requests.

This structure could create ongoing economic value for the ecosystem.

However, the proposal does not sufficiently establish:

- Expected annual numbers of paid-verification requests.
- Anticipated average realised verification fees.
- Expected annual Treasury receipts.
- The method of calculating, auditing and transferring the revenue share.
- The contractual duration and enforceability of the revenue allocation.

A percentage revenue commitment does not by itself establish that material revenue will be generated.

The separate Cardano transaction fees paid by government users should not be confused with the proposed 70% share of optional verification-service revenue.

#### 4. Social Impact

PFMS could contribute to greater transparency, evidence integrity and accountability within criminal justice procedures.

Such outcomes align with RCADA's interest in technology that supports stronger institutions and more resilient social systems.

However, the proposal's estimated **30% reduction in case-resolution times** should be treated as a projection rather than a verified outcome.

RCADA would welcome independent evidence explaining the expected relationship between evidence verification and the broader legal, procedural and institutional factors that influence case duration.

#### 5. Financial and Delivery Risk

The requested ₳9.6 million is a substantial commitment of public Treasury resources.

Milestone gating and administrator oversight reduce exposure to later undelivered work, but do not eliminate risk relating to funds already disbursed.

The most material risks include:

- Delayed or unsuccessful integration with government systems.
- Contractual dependencies involving public-sector institutions.
- Failure to complete the wider five-stage deployment.
- Insufficient protection or recovery of the initial advance.
- Uncertain revenue from optional verification services.
- Lower-than-projected usage or social outcomes.
- Security, privacy and legal risks associated with criminal justice information systems.

**Impact assessment: Significant potential public and ecosystem benefit, but material contractual, legal-integration and financial assurances remain outstanding.**

## RCADA Rationale

RCADA recognises the significant potential of the Police Force Management System (PFMS) to demonstrate government-scale adoption of Cardano while addressing a meaningful challenge within the criminal justice system: establishing reliable, independently verifiable records of evidence integrity and custody.

We acknowledge Trivolve Tech's existing Forensic Management System deployment on Cardano, which provides a foundation for the proposed expansion. The ambition to develop a reusable, open-source evidence infrastructure capable of serving public institutions also aligns with our support for practical blockchain adoption and lasting ecosystem value.

The proposed allocation of **70% of paid-verification revenue to the Cardano Treasury**, alongside government-funded operational costs, represents a constructive attempt to connect public investment with ongoing economic benefits.

However, **RCADA has identified several important questions that remain insufficiently resolved for us to provide an affirmative vote on the requested ₳9.6 million Treasury withdrawal**.

Our first concern relates to the proposed government deployment commitments.

The proposal states that the relevant Telangana government agreements exist, have been reviewed by the Cardano Foundation, and cannot be publicly disclosed due to confidentiality requirements.

We recognise that sensitive government arrangements may require confidentiality.

Nevertheless, given the significance of these commitments to the proposal's viability, we would welcome independent confirmation of their scope, enforceability, duration and the government's obligations regarding deployment, transaction fees, maintenance and ongoing infrastructure costs.

Our second concern is the relationship between PFMS and India's existing evidence-management and criminal justice infrastructure.

We would welcome a clearer explanation of how PFMS will integrate with established national and state systems, including e-Sakshya, and how its verification processes will satisfy the applicable legal requirements governing electronic evidence.

These are particularly important considerations for a system intended to support police investigations, prosecutions and court proceedings, where technical reliability, legal validity and procedural fairness must work together.

Our third concern relates to the proposed funding structure.

Although we welcome the appointment of the Cardano Foundation as administrator and the use of milestone-based disbursements, the initial **₳1.8 million kickoff payment represents 18.75% of the total Treasury request**.

This substantial advance would be released before acceptance of the first development milestone, while the independent assurer is expected to be appointed following commencement.

We would therefore appreciate greater clarity regarding the contractual protections, independently verified prerequisites, recovery mechanisms and remedies available to the Treasury should subsequent delivery fail or the project be unable to proceed as intended.

We also encourage further substantiation of the projected **1.5 million annual Cardano transactions**, the anticipated revenue from paid verification services and the proposed improvements in case-resolution efficiency.

These outcomes could create meaningful value, but their assumptions and measurement methods should be transparent and independently assessable.

**RCADA's decision to ABSTAIN reflects the project's promising potential alongside these material unanswered questions.**

We do not oppose the underlying initiative. Indeed, the prospect of providing government institutions with reliable, transparent and verifiable evidence-management infrastructure represents a compelling application of blockchain technology.

However, the scale of the requested investment requires proportionate assurance that the institutional commitments, legal integration, implementation safeguards and expected benefits are sufficiently established.

Our governance philosophy is grounded in the **generation and regeneration of environmental, social and economic sustainability**.

We welcome initiatives that can improve institutional accountability, strengthen public trust and contribute to Cardano's long-term economic resilience.

We encourage Trivolve Tech and the proposed administrator to provide further clarification of the identified concerns, particularly the government agreements, integration with existing justice systems and protections surrounding the initial advance payment.

**RCADA therefore ABSTAINS**, offering these observations as constructive feedback rather than opposition to the project's objectives.

We remain supportive of responsible government adoption initiatives that combine demonstrable public benefit with transparent governance, accountable funding and sustainable long-term value creation for the Cardano ecosystem.
